# Voiceover script — "The Body, Not the Empire"

A counter to the "America bankrolls the entire planet while its own people struggle" claim,
using the body/organism metaphor. The on-screen subtitles already carry this text; record
this narration over the top if you want a human voice. Timecodes match the 63s cut.

---

**[0:00 — The claim]**
"His argument is simple: America is bankrolling the entire planet — while its own people
struggle. Fifty billion dollars. A hundred and seventy-seven countries. He calls it a
bleeding empire. So let's actually look at the body."

**[0:08 — The heart]**
"An empire isn't a predator standing over a corpse. It's a body. The center feeds the
limbs — Rome built the roads it ruled — not out of charity, but because that's how a center
stays alive."

**[0:17 — The selfish liver]**
"Picture a liver that decides to keep everything for itself. Hoard all the blood, all the
protein. For a moment it's the richest organ in the body. Then the body dies. And a dead
body has a dead liver. Selfishness, for one part of a system, isn't strength. It's suicide."

**[0:28 — Hemorrhage vs. circulation]**
"Here's the move he's making. He's calling circulation a hemorrhage. A hemorrhage is blood
that leaves and never comes back. Circulation is blood that leaves by design — and returns
carrying oxygen. Money leaving isn't the wound. Stable markets, allies, the dollar's reserve
status: it comes back."

**[0:39 — The credit card]**
"And his best line — Americans buying groceries on a credit card — is the proof. That card
works *because* America sits at the heart of the global economy. The cheap borrowing, the
reserve currency: that's the privilege of the center. Stop the circulation, and the card
stops working too."

**[0:48 — Proportion]**
"Fifty billion sounds like the body bleeding out. It's about seven-tenths of one percent of
federal spending. One drop — not a gush."

**[0:55 — The honest close]**
"None of this means spend without limits. A healthy body regulates the flow — it doesn't
stop the heart. Measured, targeted, audited: yes. But circulation isn't charity. It's how
the center stays the center."
